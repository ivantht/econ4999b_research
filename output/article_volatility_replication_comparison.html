Comparing the article with our capital-flow volatility model

Overall assessment: The outcome, country-specific geopolitical risk (GPR), control categories and country fixed effects follow the article's general approach. However, our World Governance Indicators (WGI) interaction models are an extension, not an exact replication: the specification and effective sample differ.

Scope: This comparison concerns capital-flow volatility, not the article's separate stock-price, bond-yield or exchange-rate outcomes. Our findings below are as reported in the tagged WGI results, not a fresh re-estimation.

Sources: Article text; capital-flow volatility and WGI report; country-month panel; our summary statistics. The WGI report is located outside the repository; the relative link works while its current location is retained.

The five countries with no complete regression month are Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand, South Africa and Ukraine. The first four lack industrial production in our panel, and Ukraine lacks the policy rate. Financial-development values end in 2021-12, so the one-month lag prevents observations after 2022-01 from entering without filling.

The article describes an unbalanced panel. Its 29-country target does not prove that all 29 countries entered every individual regression.

5. Comparison of findings

The article's Table 2 reports a GPR coefficient of +0.867 for capital-flow volatility, significant at the 5% level. Our report's GPR coefficients are negative and not significant using country-clustered standard errors:

WGI specificationGPR coefficientClustered p GPR × WGI coefficientClustered p
Rule of law−0.5850.216−0.3400.038
Government effectiveness−0.6200.220−0.6300.064
Regulatory quality−0.6410.225−0.7740.117

The interaction-model GPR coefficient is the estimated GPR association at the regression's average WGI score, whereas the article's coefficient comes from a model without WGI. Their signs differ, but this comparison does not isolate the cause. The rule-of-law interaction is significant at 5% with the report's clustered standard errors; it indicates a varying association, not proof that institutions causally reduce volatility.

6. Recommended next checks

  1. Run the article's baseline without WGI on our available data. Then add WGI on the same observations to isolate the effect of the extension.
  2. Audit the portfolio-inflow series: source, units, rolling-window convention, missing months and vintage.
  3. Compare the regression sample itself: country-by-country observations, dates and missing controls.
  4. Investigate oil, central-bank independence and debt sources and transformations, where descriptive differences are visible.
  5. Confirm inference choices where the article's documentation permits; retain country-clustered results and report the limited number of clusters.
Bottom line: This is a reasonable research extension inspired by the article, but it is not yet a like-for-like replication. A no-WGI Table 2(1) benchmark on a documented common sample is the most useful next step.
