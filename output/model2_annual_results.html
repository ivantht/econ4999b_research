Model 2 on annual data

Emerging economies, 14 countries, 2002–2021, 204 country-years — one observation per country per year

Same specification, different clock. The outcome, the WGI term, the interaction, the seven controls, the country fixed effects, and the country clustering are those of the monthly Model 2. What changes is that each observation is a country-year rather than a country-month.

Aggregation. Every monthly regressor is averaged inside the calendar year. The outcome is the mean of the twelve monthly three-month rolling standard deviations, which is the same rule the quarterly file uses on three months.

Complete years only. A country-year enters when all twelve monthly volatilities are observed. Eleven country-years carrying nine or ten months are excluded from the main sample and readmitted in the robustness table.

WGI enters at its current-year value, not lagged. WGI is constant within a calendar year in the source panel, so a one-year lag would substitute last year's institutional score. Using the current-year score keeps this run as close as possible to the monthly one, where eleven of twelve months already use the current year's value. The downside is that the WGI term is not predetermined here, so it cannot be read as an exogenous institution effect.

Reading the annual run

  • The interaction is insignificant in every column, with p from 0.110 to 0.792, so the annual data give no evidence that institutional quality conditions the GPR effect.
  • GPR itself is negative everywhere and clears 5 percent once, in the government-effectiveness column (−0.3343, p = 0.021). The remaining five columns sit between p = 0.153 and p = 0.612. Unlike the monthly and quarterly runs, which put the GPR level at or near zero, the annual estimates lean negative, but one rejection in six is thin support and the other five columns do not corroborate it.
  • Government effectiveness is also the one column where WGI itself is significant (1.3133, p = 0.017), with a positive sign. Both annual rejections therefore sit in the same column and share its regressors, so they are not two independent findings. This is the same column that reached 10 percent monthly and 5 percent quarterly, which makes it the most persistent institutional result in the project — and the one most in need of a specification that can separate the level from the interaction.
  • The sample falls to 204 country-years from 2,567 country-months. Clustering is unchanged at 14 countries, so precision is limited: the interaction's standard error is at least half its point estimate in every column and exceeds the estimate itself in three.
  • Comparing coefficients across frequencies is not meaningful in magnitude. Averaging twelve months removes within-year noise and shrinks the variance of both the outcome and the regressors, so annual coefficients are not rescaled versions of the monthly ones.
  • Ukraine carries the Emerging label but contributes no observations, because it has no policy rate. That is the same exclusion as in the monthly and quarterly runs.
