The picture is essentially a null, with one exception. Volatility shows no association with debt/GDP at all (+0.008 pooled, −0.001 within, +0.008 differenced), and neither does the absolute size of the flow. The single non-trivial coefficient is the within-country correlation of the signed level (+0.147), discussed below. That is a materially different answer from the portfolio-flow series, which returned roughly +0.24 pooled.

2. Where does the flow correlation actually live?

Aggregation is doing something: the within-country association is concentrated in the signed level (+0.147), not in volatility. The sign is interpretable — higher debt goes with somewhat smaller net inflows — but the magnitude is modest, and it does not survive into volatility or into the differenced series.

Two things follow. First, the components co-move rather than offset: their volatilities correlate at +0.45 to +0.57 with each other, so aggregation does not wash out a component-level signal. Second, portfolio investment is the weakest component on this sample (+0.034 pooled, and negative on average across countries), which means the +0.24 from the model file cannot be attributed to the portfolio definition. It comes from the sample: those 15 countries include Brazil, China, Colombia, Malaysia, Poland and Türkiye, and that six-country block alone carries a within-country correlation of +0.294 on the model file's own volatility series.

4. Why the two numbers are not comparable

An additional reason to be careful: the model file's portfolio_inflow is not in the same units as the USD flow used here. Regressing one on the other implies a GDP-like denominator, so the model variable is effectively GDP-normalised while the overall flow is in levels. A pooled correlation in levels mixes a scale effect into the statistic: countries with larger economies have both bigger dollar flows and their own debt levels, so the pooled coefficient partly measures size, not behaviour. Within-country correlations are immune to this, and they are where the two series are closest.

5. Bottom line

Overall capital flows carry no debt/GDP signal in volatility terms. Pooled +0.008, within-country −0.001. Unlike portfolio flows, there is no cross-country gradient to explain.

The one non-trivial association is the signed level, within country (+0.147). It points the intuitive way — more indebted economies attract smaller net inflows — but it is weak, dispersed in sign across countries, and absent from the differenced series (+0.026).

Treat the earlier +0.24 as a portfolio-flow, model-sample statistic. It is reproducible, but it reflects that nine-country overlapping set plus the six non-overlapping economies, not the overall financial account and not a consistent panel.
