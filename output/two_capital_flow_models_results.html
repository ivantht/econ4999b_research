Two capital-flow models

Ukraine, Thailand, and China are dropped. GPR is contemporaneous and standardized within country. The other controls, including WGI, use the previous month. WGI is the calendar year of that month, and a year missing from the file stays blank. WGI is centered before it is multiplied by GPR, so the GPR coefficient is the effect at average WGI. Oil, VIX, and global EPU are logged. Each cell shows the coefficient, the country-clustered standard error in parentheses, and the 95% confidence interval. *** p<0.01, ** p<0.05, * p<0.10.

Model 1 copies the paper's capital-flow-volatility regression. The outcome is the three-month standard deviation of portfolio inflows divided by that country's average absolute portfolio inflow. The average uses every non-missing inflow, so it is a country constant, and a blank inflow stays out of the average. The unit is the country's typical monthly inflow. Industrial production and central-bank independence are in the paper's equation and are left out here because they are not in the requested regressor list.

Model 2 uses the year-over-year percent change of the same portfolio inflows, not the straight line fitted to that percent. Because a flow can change sign, the percent is 100 times the change divided by the average of the two absolute flows. A missing lag, or a zero denominator, stays blank. The percent is not logged. A positive coefficient means the flow moves toward a larger inflow. A positive WGI interaction means a higher score offsets the GPR effect.
