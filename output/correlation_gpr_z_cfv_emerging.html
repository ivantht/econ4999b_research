GPR and capital-flow volatility: emerging economies

Pearson correlation per country · monthly · January 2000 – December 2023 · 13 emerging markets, excluding China and Hungary

x is gpr_z, y is capital_flow_volatility, both current month. Because gpr_z is built as (gpr − country mean) / country standard deviation, it is a strictly increasing linear transform of gpr within each country. Pearson correlation is invariant to such transforms, so every number below is numerically identical to the one obtained from raw gpr. The standardization only changes how values are interpreted, not the correlation.
China and Hungary are excluded, and that single change flips the headline result. In the 15-country sample those two countries carry the largest correlations (+0.410 and +0.342) and together account for the entire pooled positive association. Removing them takes the pooled r from +0.135 to -0.011 and the country-mean r from +0.026 to -0.028. The 15-country table was therefore not robust: two observations out of fifteen were driving it.

What the pattern says

6 of 13 countries have a positive correlation, but the spread runs from +0.130 to -0.271. The country-level average is -0.028 and the median -0.006: with the two high-correlation economies gone, the centre of the distribution is essentially zero and very slightly negative.

Only Brazil (+0.130), Thailand (−0.143) and Ukraine (−0.271) clear the conventional threshold, and Brazil and Thailand only just, at |t| slightly above 2. The three largest remaining coefficients disagree in sign, so there is no emerging-market pattern to read off this table.

Read together with the pooled figures — -0.011 on the raw pool and -0.0174 after removing country means — the per-country scatter is consistent with there being no stable within-country link between geopolitical risk and capital-flow volatility once China and Hungary are set aside.

Method

Data: cleaned/replication_cleaned/country_month.csv, rows with group == "Emerging" and country not in {China, Hungary}. Months outside 2000-01 to 2023-12 are excluded, as are rows where either series is missing. Volatility values at or below zero cannot be logged but are kept here because this table uses Pearson on levels, not on logs. No controls, no lags, no detrending: these are raw contemporaneous associations.

The exclusion is a fragility check, not a preferred specification. China and Hungary were not selected on any institutional criterion; they were removed because they are the two outliers that generate the result.

Chart is hand-built SVG. Correlation is Pearson. Within-country figure is computed on country-demeaned values of both series. Significance uses the t approximation and is indicative only, since monthly volatility is serially correlated, which makes the effective sample smaller than the month count.
