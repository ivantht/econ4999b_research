Model 2 with capital-inflow volatility as the outcome

Emerging economies, 14 countries, 2002-02 to 2022-01, 2,567 country-months — identical sample to the portfolio-based Model 2

What changed. Only the flow that enters the three-month rolling standard deviation. The existing outcome uses portfolio inflows. The new outcome adds foreign direct investment and other investment, so the underlying flow is total capital inflow.

Everything else is held fixed. Same controls, same one-month lags, same country fixed effects, same country-clustered standard errors, same window, same Emerging filter. Both outcomes therefore use exactly 2,567 country-months and 14 countries, and the two tables are directly comparable.

One caveat on the new series. FDI and other-investment components are blank for some countries in the source file. Where they are blank the total reduces to portfolio inflows, so the new outcome differs from the existing one for 10 of 15 countries and is numerically identical for 5 (Colombia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Mexico, South Africa). Thailand is partly identical.

Reading the change

  • GPR and the WGI interaction remain insignificant in all six columns, as they were with the portfolio outcome. Widening the flow definition does not produce evidence that geopolitical risk moves capital-inflow volatility in emerging economies.
  • The one WGI dimension that clears 10 percent moves from government effectiveness (p = 0.057) to rule of law (p = 0.069). Neither threshold is crossed robustly, and both are single columns out of six.
  • The new outcome is larger on average (median ratio 1.46), because FDI and other investment add variance rather than cancel it. Coefficients are therefore not comparable in size across the two tables; only their signs and significance are.
  • Where the two series coincide the country contributes no identifying variation to the difference between specifications. 5 of 15 countries are in that position for their whole span.
