2. Standardization and fixed effects

The paper says GPR is "standardized by country". Standardizing within country sets each country's mean to zero, so adding country fixed effects on top removes almost nothing further. That makes these four cells close to only two distinct estimates, and it is why the sign does not respond to the fixed-effect choice on its own.

Verdict

The GPR coefficient is negative in 11 of 12 specifications. The only positive cells are: GPR Pooled + no FE.

The sign is therefore not a matter of the outcome's construction, the standardization choice, the window, or the fixed-effect choice taken individually. It turns positive only when GPR is pooled-standardized and country fixed effects are dropped at the same time, which sets aside two parts of the paper's stated method.

That cell is also the wrong comparison for a different reason: with no fixed effects, the coefficient is identified off cross-country differences in GPR levels, which is precisely the variation that fixed effects exist to remove.
