Reading the panels. Rows are the three flow measures, columns are countries, and every panel shares the same layout: flow on the left axis, the annual debt ratio as a stepped line on the right axis. The r= figure in each panel is that country's within-country correlation with debt/GDP for the measure being plotted, so you can compare the picture against the number directly.

Debt moves in annual steps. The line is flat within each year and jumps once, which is why it looks like a staircase rather than a curve. Any monthly or quarterly reading of it is a within-year repeat, not new information.

What the panels show

The flow series are dominated by episodes, not trends. Argentina, Turkey and Ukraine show violent swings around the 2001, 2008, 2018 and 2022 crises. Debt lines are comparatively smooth, so most of the visual co-movement, where it exists, comes from crisis quarters in which flow plunges while debt rises.

Signs genuinely differ across countries. Chile, Turkey and Indonesia turn negative on the signed flow (r between −0.46 and −0.43), while Indonesia, South Africa and Peru are strongly positive. That disagreement is the reason the pooled coefficient is close to zero while individual countries look anything but flat.

Volatility panels are the least structured. Tunisia (+0.70) and Hungary (+0.49) show clear positive slopes; Turkey and Indonesia are clearly negative. With 96 quarters per country, these are not noise, but they do not aggregate into a common pattern.
