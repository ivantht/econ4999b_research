Capital-flow volatility on GPR, the paper's controls, and WGI from the panel

The outcome is capital_flow_volatility in cleaned/replication_cleaned/country_month.csv, unchanged: the standard deviation of portfolio inflows over three months. Every control also keeps its original column in that same panel, lagged one month.

The single change is the source of WGI. It is now read from the panel's wgi_va, wgi_pv, wgi_ge, wgi_rq, wgi_rl, wgi_cc columns instead of cleaned/wgi.csv. The lagged month's value is used, matching the original lag convention. Each score is centered on its regression sample and interacted with within-country standardized GPR.

Country fixed effects. Within standard errors treat months as independent; clustered standard errors cluster by country. *** p<0.01, ** p<0.05, * p<0.10.

What the source change actually moves: nothing. The panel's WGI columns carry the same values that cleaned/wgi.csv supplied, so every coefficient below is identical to the earlier run, to the digit. Rule of law, for example, reproduces GPR = −0.5852 and WGI = −13.1173 exactly, with the same centering value 0.8828.

The sample does not expand either, and the reason is not WGI. The five countries the earlier run dropped fail on a control, not on the institutional score: Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand and South Africa have no observations at all for real industrial production, and Ukraine has none for the policy rate. Swapping where WGI comes from cannot admit a country that is still missing a required control.
