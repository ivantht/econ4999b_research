Article Variable Definitions and Construction

Source document: doc/how GRP affect FX and capital flow.md. This page summarizes each variable by (1) characteristics, (2) data source, and (3) processing/formula used by the authors.

1) Sample characteristics (applies to variables in the baseline panel setup)

  • Frequency: monthly panel (unbalanced).
  • Period: 2000:M1 to 2023:M12.
  • Countries (29): Belgium, Brazil, Canada, Chile, PRC, Colombia, Germany, Denmark, Spain, Finland, France, Hungary, Indonesia, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Malaysia, Netherlands, Philippines, Poland, Portugal, Sweden, Thailand, Türkiye, Ukraine, United States, South Africa.
  • Notes from authors: start year may vary by variable/country due to data availability; controls enter with one-period lag in Equation (2).
Baseline equation (paper Eq. 2):
y(i,t)^j = β·GPR(i,t) + γ·X(i,t−1) + GEPU(t−1) + VIX(t−1) + δ(i) + ε(i,t)

2) Variable-by-variable definition

Variable Characteristics (freq / period / coverage) How found (source in article) How processed / formula in article
Geopolitical risk (GPR index) Monthly; panel countries; enters as key regressor. Caldara and Iacoviello (2022), country-specific monthly GPR. Constructed by counting joint newspaper occurrences of geopolitical terms and country name/capital/main city; index is ratio of geopolitical articles to total articles. In regressions, described as standardized by country.
Capital flow volatility Monthly series built from monthly portfolio inflows. OECD Monthly Capital Flow Dataset (de Crescenzio and Lepers 2024). Baseline measure is rolling volatility over a 3-month window:
CFV(t) = SD(CF(t), CF(t−1), CF(t−2)).
Robustness uses 6/12-month windows and alternative GARCH/ARIMA-based volatility measures.
Real stock prices Monthly country panel outcome. OECD Main Economic Indicators / IMF IFS / BIS (as grouped in the data section). Used in logarithm form (table labels and model notation indicate log stock prices).
Long-term bond yield Monthly country panel outcome; 10-year government bond yield. OECD Main Economic Indicators / IMF IFS / BIS. Used in level percentage terms (not logged in table labels).
Exchange rate (REER) Monthly country panel outcome. OECD Main Economic Indicators / IMF IFS / BIS. Used as log of real effective exchange rate: log(REER).
Real industrial production Domestic fundamental control. IMF IFS and BIS database. Used in logarithm form in the model (real industrial production (log)).
Inflation Domestic fundamental control. IMF IFS and BIS database (CPI-based). Defined as year-on-year change in CPI; included as control (lagged by one period in Eq. 2).
Policy rate Domestic fundamental control. IMF IFS and BIS database. Included in level percentage terms; enters with one-period lag.
Oil price Global factor control. WTI crude oil price series. Used in logarithm form (Oil price (log)); enters with one-period lag in baseline setup.
VIX Global factor control. CBOE VIX index. Used as log(VIX); enters as VIX(t−1) in Eq. 2.
Global EPU Global factor control. Baker, Bloom, and Davis (2016) Economic Policy Uncertainty index. Used as log(GEPU); enters as GEPU(t−1) in Eq. 2.
Financial development index Country-specific characteristic control. IMF Financial Development Index. Index level included as control and in heterogeneity splits.
Central bank independence index Country-specific characteristic control. Romelli (2022) CBI index. Index level included as control and in heterogeneity splits.
Public debt to GDP Country-specific characteristic control. IMF World Economic Outlook. Debt-to-GDP ratio in percent, included as control.

3) Clarifications on what the article does not fully specify

  • For some variables (e.g., stock price deflator method, exact REER broad/narrow basket), the markdown notes list data repositories but not a deeper transformation pipeline.
  • The paper clearly states transformed forms in the regression framework/tables (e.g., log(REER), log stock, log oil, log VIX, log GEPU, log industrial production) and the one-period lag structure for controls.
